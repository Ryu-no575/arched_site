HOW TO READ IT

Knowledge Atlas

Map · tap each item

DirectionThe subject area of a branch.

Fields occupy broad neighbourhoods, with overlaps and connections between them. Direction is a navigation aid, not a strict boundary or a measure of progress.

HeightHistorical guide bands, not exact dates.

Era bands provide orientation. Some topics sit near subject anchors rather than their historical band, so height cannot date a fact. Rotation also changes what appears above or below. Use the period filter and inspector; Tapestry provides the dated view.

Distance from centerCore ideas to specific knowledge.

Broad subject anchors organise more specific topics around them. Local spacing keeps dots apart, so distance is approximate; it is never a score or a measure of importance.

Dots · tap each item

Large dotMajor field or core concept.

These are the anchors of the Atlas. They organize smaller topics even before you answer a question.

Small dotA source-backed fact or question target.

A dot represents a recorded fact or topic. Not every dot has an available quiz in your chosen language. Select it to see the source and any Practice or Related question buttons.

Very faint dot · Details onlyOne possible General Knowledge question target.

Thousands of tightly scoped study targets extend the map beyond the current source bank. Some come from the uploaded PDF topic index, with page references in the inspector. A faint candidate is a topic to verify and develop; it is not a reviewed fact, an available question, or evidence of your mastery. Tap to inspect the possible question angle.

Bright whiteKnowledge strengthened by a correct answer.

A correct answer turns the tested fact white. It dims gradually with time, even while the app is closed. A correct review restores the glow; an incorrect answer stays dim. Simply opening a dot does not count as recall. Progress is retained: brightness is a visual review cue, not a measured probability of remembering.

Cyan glowRelated concepts revealed by your answers.

A correct answer may reveal nearby named entities or supported concepts. A cyan glow means unlocked knowledge, not that you have answered a question about it.

GoldCarefully unlocked frontier knowledge.

A predicted, relevant next concept appears only after enough support from solved knowledge. Gold is a study lead, not a mastered fact.

Field colours identify unstudied topics. White marks correctly recalled facts; their glow fades. Cyan and gold show related study leads, not quiz success. Selection and hovering add a temporary highlight.

Lines · tap each item

CyanPrerequisite or hierarchy.

Shows a broad-to-specific relationship, such as a field connecting to a topic or a foundational concept leading to a more specific one.

PurpleShared named entity or concept.

Connects nodes referring to the same identifiable person, work, place, or concept. It indicates a concrete shared subject.

BlueRelated concepts within a subject.

Connects a source topic with a recorded adjacent concept, such as a Doric metope and its neighboring triglyph. The connection is conceptual; it does not claim that one causes the other.

Yellow dashedHistorical sequence.

Connects events or works through a meaningful time order. It tells you what came earlier or later, not what caused the next event.

GreenCross-field bridge.

Connects knowledge from different fields when the relation has supporting evidence, such as an architectural movement and its artistic counterpart.

Gray dottedHistorical layer or structure.

Places a node within a historical layer or another structural grouping. It provides context without claiming a direct prerequisite.

In Details, tap a faint dot to inspect its candidate question target. Selected connections may turn white; selection is not learning progress.

Tapestry

Read

Follow each coloured country or regional thread down through time. Events, works and their creators sit beside the same line. The gently curved threads stay still when scrolling stops. Labels and cards follow the same geometry. Pictures have their own rows and never cover events. Knowledge contains every current Atlas point, including study targets awaiting verification. Period knowledge is a study grouping, not a claim of exact dates. Space is reading emphasis, not a measure of political power.

Tap an event to draw its specific relationships. Dotted lines lead to related entries outside the visible dates; tap one to travel there. The relationship label explains the connection.

Threads represent historical regions and cultural associations, not continuous modern states. A cultural association does not establish where a work was made. Unspecified settings remain under Across borders. Periods are reading guides, not state lifespans. Event dates remain on entries. Knowledge cards show approximate study periods, not dates when a concept was invented. Dense years take more room, so vertical distance is not proportional to elapsed time. Coverage currently extends to 2011. Background colours show approximate overlapping traditions, not national boundaries. People adds creator lifelines to this same date axis, in their principal working region. Circle: birth/start; end bar: death/end; dashed: recorded activity, not lifespan. Dense dates are expanded for reading. Select a contemporary to highlight their line and work markers. Career moves are listed where documented; the line does not assert continuous residence.
